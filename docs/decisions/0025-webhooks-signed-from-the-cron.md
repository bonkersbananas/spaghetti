# 0025. Webhooks are queued in D1, sent by the existing cron, signed with HMAC-SHA256 under a secret kept encrypted

Status: Accepted

**Decision:** A person adds a webhook (https address, kinds of change, optionally an agent as a label). Every change in the change feed that it wants becomes one row in `webhook_deliveries` (a unique index on webhook and `seq` makes fan-out idempotent) and is POSTed as JSON in the shape of `GET /api/changes`. The 5-minute cron that already runs the nudges does two things: turn new changes into deliveries (each webhook keeps a `cursor` into the feed) and send what is due. A failed delivery is retried after 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h and 12 h; after seven tries (about 20 hours, never past a day) it is `failed`, and the webhook is marked **failing** with the last error until a later delivery gets a 2xx. A test event and "Retry" send at once, from the request itself. Targets are checked with the link-preview rules (https, public name, DNS over HTTPS, no redirects, a 10 s timeout, the receiver's answer is never read), when the webhook is made and again on every delivery.

**Signing:** `Spaghetti-Signature: v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256>` over `<Spaghetti-Timestamp>.<body>`, keyed with a per-webhook secret (`whsec_` and 32 random bytes), the scheme Stripe and Standard Webhooks made familiar: a timestamp in the signed text lets receivers refuse replays, and the version prefix leaves room to change it.

**How the secret is stored:** an HMAC key must be recoverable to sign, so unlike agent tokens it can't be a hash. It is encrypted with AES-GCM under a key derived (HKDF-SHA256) from the Worker secret `WEBHOOK_SECRET_KEY`, or `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` when that isn't set, so a copy of the database (a backup, an export) can't sign anything. It is shown once when the webhook is made; the API and the app only ever have its first ten characters. It is never logged, and neither are signatures or payloads. The Worker itself can of course decrypt it: whoever can read the Worker's secrets can sign. Rotating the key makes stored secrets unreadable: those webhooks fail until they are added again, so set `WEBHOOK_SECRET_KEY` (docs/deploy.md) rather than lean on the VAPID key, which is replaced when push is redone.

**Why the cron and D1, not Cloudflare Queues:** Queues needs the paid plan and a queue created on the account (a dashboard step like R2's), a producer and a consumer binding, and a second place to look when something is stuck; this project runs the same on workerd, in Docker and locally, and Queues doesn't run in all of those. A table the cron drains does, is visible on the Agents page as it is ("recent deliveries"), and costs one indexed read when idle. The price is latency: a delivery leaves up to five minutes after the change (a lease on `next_at` keeps overlapping runs from sending one twice, and each run sends at most 40 deliveries, eight at a time). If that becomes too slow, a write could also kick the same drain in `waitUntil`; nothing about the tables would change.

**What is sent, and what isn't:** only the change (ids, entity, action, actor, time), never titles, notes or photos, so a webhook receiver learns nothing the change feed doesn't already say and nothing personal is stored in `webhook_deliveries.payload`. Receivers fetch the thing with their token. Delivery is at least once and may be out of order across retries: `deliveryId` to dedupe, `seq` to order.

**People only:** webhooks are managed by signed-in people, never by agent tokens (`person_only`); a webhook belongs to the person who made it. Deleting one has Undo ([0006](0006-soft-deletes-with-undo.md)); finished deliveries are kept a week.

**Left out:** editing a webhook (remove and add), per-webhook kinds beyond the feed's entities, a filter by actor or item, batching several changes in one request, and delivering from a write instead of the cron.
