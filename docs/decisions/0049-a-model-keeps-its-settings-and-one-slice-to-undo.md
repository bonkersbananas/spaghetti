# 0049. A model keeps its slicing settings, is sliced again in the background, and keeps one slice to undo

Status: Accepted

**Decision:** A model (decision 0046) keeps the slicing settings people chose for it (decision 0047's shape, whole, in the file row's `slicing`; null until someone changes one), and each slice keeps the settings it was made with (`slice.settings.slicing`). Its next slice uses its own settings, else what its slice was made with, else the workshop's defaults: Spaghetti's (0.20 mm Standard, no supports) with a spool from the Pantry (the first loaded one, PLA before other materials, else the shelf; the stand-in only when the Pantry has no spool with filament on it), a plate the Printer page lists and the main nozzle (`shared/slicing/pantry.ts`). "Slice with…" on the model's card opens its settings: a tall sheet on phones, its result at the foot; on desktop a popover hanging below the card, which shows the result. A tap there changes the model at once (`PATCH /api/files/:id`, people only, after a 450 ms pause so a few quick taps are one write and one slice) and slices it again on that device in the background. The slice there stays in view, its picture, figures and download, until the new one is saved with `keep=previous`, when the server copies the one it replaces to `files/<id>/previous` and `previous-thumb` and the row's `previous_slice`. The card then says what changed, "1 h 12 min → 48 min · 14 g → 11 g", with an Undo that brings the kept slice back with its settings (`POST /api/files/:id/sliced/restore`), at once, without slicing. One step: the slice it undid isn't kept, and a slice saved without `keep` drops the kept one.

**Why:** Carl Oscar asked (Nov 13) for settings people set easily, with the result to see and an undo. Settings kept on the model mean the next "Slice it" or the Rust slicer slices it the same way, whoever asks; settings only in a sheet would be gone with the tab. Slicing takes 10 seconds to minutes on a phone: an empty card meanwhile would hide what is known, and "Stop" can put things back. An undo that slices again would take as long as the change did; keeping the one slice it replaced makes it instant, and costs at most one more copy of a sliced file per model, which goes with it. A history of slices was more than anyone asked for.

**Consequences:**

- R2 holds up to two more parts per model (`previous`, `previous-thumb`); the purge removes them with the file, and a slice saved without `keep` removes them at once.
- A re-slice whose sliced file goes in parts (over 32 MB, [0048](0048-big-files-go-in-parts.md)) lands in the bucket over the slice it replaces before it is saved, so that slice can't be kept: it is saved without `keep` (the server ignores `keep` with `uploaded`), and the card shows what changed without an Undo. A staging part for the upload would lift this if big re-slices turn out to need an undo.
- The device where a setting was changed does the slicing. Another device sees the new settings through the change feed and the new slice once it is saved; a tab closed mid-slice leaves the card saying "Not sliced with its new settings yet", with "Slice it". The story (MCP's `get_build`) says what a model was sliced with, in full, and what it will be sliced with next.
- Agents read a model's settings (`file.slicing`, `slice.settings.slicing`, `file.previous`) but can't change them or undo, as they can't slice.
- The before → after and its Undo live on the device that made the change, until the next change or a reload; the server's kept slice is what makes the undo work, not a promise to offer it everywhere.
- The help sits where it applies: a floating part's caution offers "Supports where needed" in one tap, a model too big for the plate "Scale to 78% and slice" (and "Fit the plate" by its size under More), and the filament's caption says where the picked spool is loaded.
- New pieces of the design system: `ChoiceTiles` (big choices with a picture and their effect in words), `SlicingGlyphs`, `BeforeAfter`, and a `step` and `format` on `Stepper`; the styleguide's Slicing section shows them.
